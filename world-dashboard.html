176 of ~195 sovereign states have data; a handful of the smallest microstates have no shape in the source map and are skipped. Five shapes on the map (Greenland, Puerto Rico, New Caledonia, the Falklands, and France's overseas territories) are non-sovereign or non-mainland territories, shown in flat gray with no data. Every layer is a snapshot from 2025-26 research, not a live feed, and several figures (North Korea's GDP, several countries' military budgets) are flagged in their own panels as unofficial estimates. See "Where this data comes from" below for sourcing and every simplification made.

Where this data comes from, and its limits
  • Geography: "Simple World Map" by Al MacDonald & Fritz Lekschas (based on a Wikimedia Commons map), CC BY-SA 3.0 — 180 of the world's ~195 sovereign states have a drawable shape; the rest are microstates too small for this low-resolution map (Vatican, San Marino, Monaco, Liechtenstein, Andorra, most Pacific and several Caribbean microstates). France's mainland shape is separated from its six overseas departments/territories (French Guiana, Réunion, Guadeloupe/Martinique, St Pierre & Miquelon, French Polynesia), which are shown neutral rather than inheriting mainland France's data. Somaliland is shown separately on the map but its figures are Somalia's national figures; Western Sahara has no separate shape and is folded into Morocco, which administers most of the territory.
  • Population & GDP: current UN/World Bank population and IMF nominal GDP estimates (Worldwide via Worldometers/Wikipedia). The map colors by density (population ÷ land area) and GDP per capita rather than raw totals — raw totals mostly just re-show which countries are big; density and per-capita reveal patterns totals hide. North Korea's GDP is a rough unofficial estimate (~$20B) shown in a distinct color rather than placed on the ranked scale, since North Korea publishes no real economic data.
  • Religion: Pew Research Center's Global Religious Landscape data, simplified to a three-way Christian/Muslim/other split. Several countries have a much larger unaffiliated, Hindu, Buddhist, or traditional-religion component than that split alone suggests; India, China, and most of mainland Southeast Asia fall in "no majority" for exactly this reason.
  • Terrain: not real satellite imagery — a stylized shading of each country's single dominant biome, based on standard biogeography. Large countries spanning several biomes (the US, China, Brazil, Russia, Australia, and about 20 others) get a "also has..." note in their panel naming the biomes the single dominant type leaves out.
  • Nuclear status: the five NPT-recognized weapons states; four undeclared-but-armed states (India, Pakistan, North Korea, Israel); six states hosting another country's weapons (NATO nuclear-sharing plus Belarus since 2023); three that built or inherited weapons and gave them up (South Africa, Ukraine, Kazakhstan).
  • Security blocs: a picker rather than one color per country, since real membership overlaps (Kazakhstan is both CSTO and SCO). Covers NATO, CSTO, SCO, Five Eyes, the GCC, and standing US bilateral mutual-defense treaties (distinct from NATO). Regional economic blocs (the AU, ASEAN, EU as a security actor) aren't covered — this tracks security commitments specifically.
  • Critical minerals: USGS 2023 global production-share data. Only countries with a genuinely dominant share of a specific mineral are colored; everyone else is neutral, not zero.
  • Military: SIPRI's 2025 top-40 spenders (exact figures) plus IISS/Global Firepower personnel data for the top 15 armies, extended to the rest of the world using the same evidence standard as everywhere else in this tool. Ten countries have no standing military at all — a distinct category, not the bottom of the scale. Roughly half the smaller states have no reliable public budget figure; those show a tier estimated from population, GDP, and regional context rather than an invented number.
  • Great-power alignment: based on documented foreign troop/base presence and each country's dominant arms supplier, not on bloc membership alone — a country can be in NATO or SCO and still have no single patron (Nigeria, diversified across the US, Russia, Turkey and China, is a clean example). The US, China, Russia, India, and Iran are marked as "great power" rather than classified as anyone's client, since they're the patrons this layer measures other countries against. Most of the world (85 of 176 countries here) has no dominant single patron and is marked non-aligned/mixed — that's the honest default, not a gap in the data.
  • This is a snapshot from 2025–2026 research, not a live feed, and reflects a huge number of individual judgment calls at this scale — corrections welcome.
