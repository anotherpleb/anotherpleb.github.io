Line of sight is a straight reference line — just geometry, no physics. The other two are curves: "aimed straight at target" uses the line-of-sight angle as the barrel angle, but gravity pulls the bullet off that straight line for the whole flight, so it lands short and low. "Corrected" starts at a steeper angle — enough above the line of sight that gravity's pull brings it down exactly on target instead.

Model: numerical simulation with quadratic air drag (constant drag coefficient ≈0.45, not a full Mach-dependent G1/G7 curve). Bullet weight and diameter come from the selected cartridge; drag scales accordingly, so lighter/blunter bullets (like .22 LR) lose velocity and range faster than heavier, more streamlined ones. Absolute drop won't match a certified ballistic calculator, but the relative behavior — and the elevation/holdover effect this tool exists to show — is physically grounded.
