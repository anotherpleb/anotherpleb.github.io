Where this data comes from, and its limits
  • Satellite / Terrain: not real satellite imagery (this is a single static file with no live map data) — it's a stylized shading of each country's single dominant biome (Sahara desert, Sahel semi-arid belt, savanna/grassland, Congo Basin and coastal rainforest, East African highlands, Mediterranean coastal scrub), based on standard African biogeography. Large countries that straddle several biomes (e.g. Nigeria, Angola, Ethiopia) are simplified to one.
  • Population & GDP: current UN/World Bank population estimates and IMF nominal GDP estimates, via Wikipedia's compiled country lists.
  • Religion: Pew Research Center's 2020 religious-composition estimates. Shown here as a simplified three-way split (Christian / Muslim / other) — several countries have a much larger "traditional African religion" or unaffiliated component than that split alone suggests; where that matters, it's called out in the country panel.
  • Military: defense expenditure and active-personnel figures are from SIPRI and the IISS/Global Firepower index where publicly reported — mostly the larger militaries. Roughly half of the continent's smaller states have no reliable public figures; those are shown as "limited data" rather than an invented number, with a relative 1–5 tier based on the best available evidence.
  • Great-power alignment: based on concrete, documented things — foreign troop/base presence (US/AFRICOM, France, Russia's Africa Corps/Wagner, China's Djibouti base), each country's dominant arms supplier (SIPRI Arms Transfers Database), and, for China specifically, dominant-supplier equipment data and the Boston University Chinese Loans to Africa database for defense-sector lending. China's continent-wide role is overwhelmingly economic rather than military, so it's only shown as the "aligned" power for the handful of countries (Zimbabwe, Zambia, Tanzania) where it is clearly the dominant military patron with no serious competing supplier — not as a general marker of Chinese trade or infrastructure ties, which run far wider than this layer shows. Countries with no dominant single patron by any of these measures are labeled "non-aligned / mixed" rather than forced into a bloc. France's own African basing has shrunk sharply since 2023 — it now holds only Djibouti and Gabon.
  • In two countries (Liberia and the Central African Republic) the source survey's Christian and Muslim shares added up to slightly over 100% (a multiple-affiliation reporting artifact); both were proportionally rescaled to sum to 100%.
  • This is a snapshot as of 2025–2026 research, not a live feed. Borders and shapes are a simplified low-resolution map, not a survey-accurate one; Western Sahara is shown as part of Morocco (which administers most of the territory) rather than as its own entry, and the self-declared Somaliland region is shown separately on the map but its data is Somalia's national data.
